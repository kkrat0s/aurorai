## The story

**A phone can detect a spike. AuroraSense investigates what the evidence permits us to conclude.**

The opening supplies a physical question, the three tabs supply context, and the real Muse Spark audit supplies the decisive agent action. The scientific result is valuable even when it remains inconclusive. Present AuroraSense as a working local research prototype with a testable path toward citizen space-weather science.

The public competition description emphasizes creativity, technical ambition and working execution for the Indie Developer track, and physical/scientific capability for Push the Frontier. This story provides evidence for each, without inventing scoring weights. [Official event description](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon)

## Exact voice-over

Read only the quoted paragraphs. Do not read the headings or production notes.

### 0:00–0:18 · Cold open

> A phone can detect a magnetic spike. The hard part is knowing what it means. Here, a controlled local disturbance is flagged. One phone is not enough to establish a regional storm.

### 0:18–0:30 · Product promise

> AuroraSense connects space-weather forecasts, ground measurements, and a Meta-powered scientific coordinator, so people can explore the aurora and investigate what their sensors actually recorded.

### 0:30–0:50 · Storm Track

> Start with Storm Track. We're replaying the May twenty twenty-four storm using NASA's archived arrival predictions: ten submissions from seven methods. Move the clock to inspect what had been submitted. The spread shows disagreement, not a guaranteed arrival or severity.

### 0:50–1:09 · Aurora Map

> Now switch to Live. Aurora Map shows NOAA's modeled auroral activity with its actual valid time. Darkness adds viewing context; missing clouds stay unknown. Modeled activity, magnetic disturbance, and a confirmed sighting are three different kinds of evidence.

### 1:09–1:32 · Ground Truth

> Ground Truth brings the experiment to your phone: explicit consent, foreground recording, stop, and export. One ten-minute iPhone recording captured five thousand nine hundred eighty-five samples and passed our fixed quality checks. Historical USGS measurements provide separate storm context. Passing phone quality checks does not establish a solar cause.

### 1:32–2:13 · Real Meta run

> Here's where Meta acts. This is a recorded, genuine Muse Spark one point three run, with public USGS data and no phone sessions loaded. Spark selected tools to inspect coverage, retrieve reference evidence, and request regional validation. It then requested an opt-in observation. Delivery wasn't configured, so the request was blocked: nobody was contacted. The deterministic validator returned inconclusive, and the report preserved that conclusion. Seven permitted tools and bounded model calls keep the workflow inspectable. The model chooses investigative steps; the scientific validator controls the verdict.

### 2:13–2:32 · Research

> We also tested a thirty-minute ground-field model on held-out historical events. The history model's mean absolute error was slightly worse than persistence, and it lost to the matched simpler model. We report that result. Phone-driven forecasting gains remain an open research question.

### 2:32–2:50 · Why and close

> Tap Why to follow solar-wind energy through magnetic reconnection and particle precipitation, to oxygen and nitrogen emitting auroral light. Our next experiment is independent, synchronized phone and reference measurements. AuroraSense: see the storm, question the signal, follow the evidence.

## Voice and edit direction

- Use the builder's natural voice: curious in the opening, concrete through the demonstration, measured on “inconclusive,” and optimistic about the next experiment. Pronounce AuroraSense “uh-ROAR-uh sense,” Muse “myooz,” NOAA “NO-ah,” NASA “NAS-uh,” and USGS as the letters.
- Leave a brief pause after “what it means,” “nobody was contacted,” and “We report that result.” Allow the viewer to read the flagged spike, blocked action, verdict and score table.
- Capture a 1920 × 1080 landscape master at 30 fps, with the vertical phone framed alongside a readable crop when needed. These are editing recommendations, not verified competition encoding rules.
- Keep text large enough to read on a phone. Reframe important panels rather than shrinking a full scrolling page into the video. Use one deliberate cursor action per beat and remove loading waits. Caption any accelerated waiting or time-lapse.
- Add accurate English captions, preferably both an uploadable caption track and readable burned-in captions if the host permits. Use at most two lines at once and check that captions do not cover timestamps, units or provenance. The project plan calls for narration/captions; current external mandates are distinguished in the requirements file.
- Prefer clean speech with no music. Use music or additional aurora footage only when its public-video rights are established and credited. Product screenshots and original conceptual animation already provide sufficient visuals. The approved wireframe contains fictional readings and is not demo footage.
- Preserve source, unit, frame, quality, relevant time and provenance on every scientific shot, in the UI or a readable attached caption. Measurement, forecast issue, valid, availability and retrieval times must not be silently interchanged.
- Keep the long Carrington/device-impact intro, uncertain object guesses, synthetic volunteer workflow, code scrolling and optional integrations out of this cut. The script covers the project's main scientific workflow; fuller architecture and secondary features belong in the written submission.

## Recording readiness and truthful substitutions

1. **Opening:** the October 28 export and recomputed aggregate report support a real recorded perturbation, not independently filmed hardware operation. Prefer actual physical-device footage. If unavailable, present the dated owner-provided record/report with the same narration; crop to the flagged interval or statistics rather than a dense document. Use the October 28 date only for that export; newly filmed measurements need their actual date and an actual matching QC outcome. Never create a synthetic trace to fill this shot. Do not infer the particular object or the cause of every flag.
2. **Accepted phone:** use the October 31 record, not either rejected October 28 run. Acquisition acceptance is not a noise-floor, calibrated sensitivity, regional-storm or aurora claim. Keep the phone and 2024 observatory dates visibly separate.
3. **NOAA availability:** refresh and inspect real timestamps before filming. If the source fails, show the honest stale/unavailable state or a timestamped source snapshot. Alternate first map sentence: “Aurora Map preserves NOAA's modeled activity and its source time; when the source is stale or unavailable, that state stays visible.” Never silently substitute an old snapshot as current.
4. **Actual Meta:** the recorded run used zero phone sessions, loaded one genuine professional reference, and could align zero references to phone observations. It did not analyze the cold-open recording, call local phone QC, contact volunteers, receive new measurements, or run a completed invitation-to-result loop. Do not splice the synthetic workflow into the recorded audit.
5. **Current native analysis:** the newest capture-analysis screen is implemented but awaits a fresh physical-device visual check. Use it only after a genuine session renders successfully. Its per-session analysis is local deterministic processing, not a Spark call. The dated evidence reports support the script without depending on this newer screen.
6. **Hosted services:** Supabase integration/schema exists, but hosted Auth/RLS/private ingestion/deletion and participant delivery are unverified. The script makes no claim of a deployed participant network. No public deployment is needed to prepare this recording package.
7. **Future work:** synchronized independent sites, paired reference validation, calibrated optical forecasts and measured phone forecasting gains are future experiments. Broomfield is not accepted historical phone-storm evidence; EZIE testing is deferred; Android exports do not establish physical Android validation.
